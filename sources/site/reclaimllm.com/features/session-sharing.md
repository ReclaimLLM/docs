# Share AI coding sessions 
with full control.

Send a time-limited, revocable link to any collaborator — client, colleague, or reviewer. They get a read-only view of the full session. No account required on their end. Enterprise workspaces also enforce role-based visibility: admins see the org, team leads see their teams, and members need explicit shares to see each other's work.

Owner controls

## Shared. Not handed over.

◎

### Recipient-specific links

Each share is tied to a named email address. You can see exactly who you shared with, when they viewed it, and revoke access for each recipient independently.

◷

### Automatic expiry

Every link has a hard expiry — 24 hours, 7 days, or 30 days. Once it expires, the link stops working without any action from you. There is no such thing as a share that stays active forever.

◁

### Instant revocation

Revoke any share at any time and the link stops working immediately. The recipient gets a notification. Your audit record is preserved.

◈

### Sensitive content warnings

Before you send a link, RCLM flags sessions that its AI identified as potentially containing sensitive content — credentials, personal data, proprietary code. You decide whether to share, not a rule you forgot to write.

Recipient experience

## A clean view. Nothing more.

Recipients open a read-only view of the full session — transcript, tool calls, and file diffs — without needing an account, a login, or access to any of your other data.

They cannot edit, export, or reshare. The link works until it expires or you revoke it, and nothing else about your account is visible.

Account requiredNone — link works in any browser

TranscriptFull conversation, tool calls, and file diffs — read-only

File changesBefore-and-after diffs for coding sessions captured with hooks

Session metadataModel, timestamps, and duration — no internal identifiers

Edits or exportsNot available — recipients can read, owners control everything else

Link lifetimeExpires on the date the owner set; revocable before that

Enterprise visibility

## Sharing is explicit. Org visibility is role-based.

Enterprise users do not all get default access to each other's sessions. RCLM separates operational visibility from peer-to-peer sharing so organizations can audit AI work without turning every member into an org-wide viewer.

A member can open another member's session only when that session is shared with them. Admin and team-lead access follows enterprise role boundaries.

Org adminCan see all sessions in the organization

Admins are responsible for org-wide governance, audit, retention, and security review.

Team leadCan see sessions for members of their assigned team

Team leads need team-level review without getting default access to unrelated teams.

MemberCan see their own sessions, plus another member's session only when it is explicitly shared

Peer access stays opt-in through session sharing instead of becoming broad org visibility.

Why it matters

## Context travels with the session, not around it.

### Hand off a debugging session mid-stream

Share the exact state of a session — the prompts, the agent's tool calls, the file changes — so a colleague can pick up where you left off without you describing it from memory.

### Show a client what the agent actually did

Share a read-only view of a coding session with a client or stakeholder. They see the full transcript without needing an account, a login, or access to your internal tools.

### Async code review with full AI context

Send the session alongside the code, not just the code. Reviewers see what was tried, what was rejected, and why the final approach is the one that landed.
